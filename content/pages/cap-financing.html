The problem

41% of SME trade finance applications rejected

The global trade finance gap reached $2.5 trillion in 2025, and banks rejected 41% of SME trade finance applications (ADB, January 2026). In food, the gap is felt at every stage: farmers cannot fund inputs, processors cannot fund raw materials, and traders cannot fund shipments while they wait for buyers to pay. Lenders say no not because the trades are bad, but because they cannot see or verify them. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

Invisible trades

Lenders cannot see a reliable record of who is trading, what and with whom.

One bank at a time

Businesses apply to banks one by one, repeating the same paperwork.

Too few funding options

SMEs rarely access supply chain finance, Islamic finance or institutional investors.

Use case · Illustrative example

Gujarat groundnut processor funds a record season

A groundnut processor in Gujarat has export orders from verified buyers in Malaysia and the UAE but not enough working capital to buy the harvest. It sends one pre-shipment finance request to several licensed lenders from its T57 deal file. Two lenders compete, and it accepts the lower-cost offer. Buyer payments go into escrow at delivery and repay the lender automatically, and the processor's completed trades strengthen its next request.

Why T57

We believe that a sound food trade should be financeable, whatever the size of the business behind it.

T57 does not lend

Finance comes from licensed banks, finance companies, Islamic finance providers and investors, who make their own decisions.

AI advises, people decide

AI helps prepare and check deal files. Lenders decide, and any adverse decision by T57 about a member is reviewed by a person.

FAQ

Common questions

Who provides the finance?

Licensed banks, finance companies, Islamic finance providers and, where permitted, institutional investors. T57 does not lend.

What do lenders see?

Only the deal file you choose to share: your T57 Passport, the contract, counterparties, documents and trade history.

How does repayment work?

In most structures, the end buyer's payment goes into escrow and repays the lender at settlement, before the balance is released to you.
