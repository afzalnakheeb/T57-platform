T57 solves the information gap between large trading houses and everyone else, by building price, supply, credit and trade data into the platform where food is bought and sold.

The problem

Trading houses see more than everyone else

Food prices are rising again. The FAO Food Price Index averaged 136.0 points in September 2026, up 5.8% on a year earlier (FAO, October 2026) and at its highest level since late 2022. Large traders follow these moves with paid terminals and analyst teams. Most food SMEs rely on calls, rumors and last month's invoices. They quote too low, buy too late, choose counterparties without checking their history and enter new markets blind. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

Pricing blind

No reliable, current price for the products and origins being traded.

Unknown counterparties

No easy way to check a buyer's or supplier's credit and trade history.

Missed signals

Supply shocks and demand shifts are seen only after they hit.

Use case · Illustrative example

Dubai sugar trader times a purchase and checks a new supplier

A Dubai sugar trader sees on T57's price index that refined sugar prices have started rising, and the forecast points to tighter supply. A new supplier in Brazil offers a competitive price. The trader checks its credit report and port trade history on T57, uses AI negotiation support to set a counter-offer based on recent trades, and closes the deal before prices climb further.

FAQ

Common questions

Where does T57's data come from?

Public market and trade sources, licensed data partners, and anonymized, aggregated data from trades on T57.

Is my trade data shared with other members?

No. Individual trades are never shown. They contribute only to anonymized, aggregated statistics.

Is intelligence included in membership?

Core intelligence is included. Higher membership tiers include deeper data, credit reports and forecasts.
