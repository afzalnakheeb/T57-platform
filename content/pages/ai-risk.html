T57's risk AI solves the scattered, late view of trade risk, by scoring counterparties, corridors and supply origins, and showing them as live heatmaps for members, lenders and governments.

The problem without AI

Why this is hard without AI

A single food trade can fail for many reasons: a buyer in financial trouble, a sanctioned owner, a port strike, a closed strait, a drought or a sudden export ban. Each risk is tracked, if at all, by a different person in a different place. Lenders and insurers price risk without seeing the trade. Governments see supply threats only after they become shortages. Without a combined view, risks are found too late to act on.

How the AI works

Data in, a small local model, a person decides

Model typeRisk-scoring models for counterparties, corridors and origins, plus event detection from news and data feeds. Results are aggregated into geographic and portfolio heatmaps.
Data inputsVerified member data and trade history on T57. Compliance screening results. Credit data from partners, with consent. Port, shipping and route data. Weather and climate data. Export and import policy changes. Supply status forecasts.
OutputRisk scores with the main reasons behind them, heatmaps by region, product and route, and alerts when a risk rises above a set level.

Use case · Illustrative example

Riyadh trading house protects its portfolio

Before

A Riyadh trading house with 20 open trades across six origins found out about a port strike and a buyer's payment troubles from emails, days apart and too late.

With T57 AI

Its T57 heatmap turns one origin port amber on strike reports and flags rising risk on one buyer from late payments recorded on T57. The trader reroutes two shipments, moves that buyer to escrow and adds trade credit cover, before either risk causes a loss.

FAQ

Common questions

Can I see my own risk score?

Yes. Members can see the scores that relate to them and the main reasons, and can ask for a review.

Who else can see risk heatmaps?

Lenders and insurers see risk only for the trades you share with them. Governments see aggregated, anonymized views.
