TodayWith T57

T57 strengthens food security by connecting verified producers and buyers across borders, and by turning verified trade data into early warning of where supply is at risk.

The problem

Import-dependent countries are exposed

645Mpeople faced hunger in 2025 (SOFI 2026)
136.0FAO Food Price Index, September 2026, up 5.8% on a year earlier
20–45%of key agrifood inputs pass through the Strait of Hormuz (FAO, April 2026)

In 2025, 645 million people faced hunger, and about 520 million are still projected to in 2030 (SOFI 2026). The FAO Food Price Index reached 136.0 in September 2026, 5.8% higher than a year earlier and the highest since November 2022. Import-dependent countries are exposed: in April 2026, FAO warned that 20 to 45% of key agrifood inputs depend on passage through the Strait of Hormuz. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

Late information

Governments see supply problems only after prices rise.

Narrow sourcing

Importers depend on a few origins and intermediaries.

Excluded producers

Small producers cannot reach the markets that need their food.

How T57 supports food security

Connect, see, act, include

  1. 01

    Connect supply and demand

    Verified producers in surplus regions meet verified buyers in import-dependent markets.

  2. 02

    See supply status

    Verified trades build a live view of supply by origin: surplus, stable, at risk or shortage.

  3. 03

    Act early

    When an origin is at risk, buyers find verified alternative suppliers and routes.

  4. 04

    Include small producers

    Cooperatives and smallholders join through assisted onboarding, adding supply and earning more.

Use case · Illustrative example

A Gulf importer switches rice origin before shelves are affected

A Riyadh rice importer sees on T57 that supply from its usual origin has moved to "at risk". It finds verified exporters in two other founding-market origins with surplus, compares landed costs and secures shipments before its stock runs low.

FAQ

Common questions

Is T57 a government system?

No. T57 is an independent platform that governments and institutions can use, with permissioned data.

What data does a government see?

Aggregated, permissioned data on supply, prices and trade, never individual members' data without consent.

Does T57 work only for Muslim-majority countries?

No. T57 serves any food business or government in its markets, and works closely with IOFS and the OIC.
