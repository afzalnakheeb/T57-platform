T57 solves the high cost and delay of moving money across borders, by giving verified food businesses any-currency accounts and transfers from licensed partners, linked to their trades.

The problem

Moving money costs too much and takes too long

Food trade crosses currencies at every step. An Indian exporter may sell in dirhams, pay freight in dollars and buy packaging in rupees. Each conversion and transfer costs money and time. Even in personal remittances, the most studied market, sending money still costs 4.6% on average digitally and 7.3% through non-digital channels (IFAD, Sending Money Home 2026). For businesses, hidden spreads, intermediary bank charges and multi-day delays add to the cost of every trade. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

Hidden costs

Exchange spreads and intermediary fees are rarely shown upfront.

Slow transfers

Payments routed through correspondent banks can take days.

Currency mismatch

Businesses are forced to convert at bad times because they cannot hold the currencies they use.

Use case · Illustrative example

Kenyan tea cooperative pays members after a sale to Dubai

A tea cooperative in Kenya sells to a verified Dubai blender and receives payment in US dollars into its T57 account. It converts what it needs to Kenyan shillings at a rate shown upfront, holds the rest in dollars for its next equipment purchase, and pays its members in one bulk transfer. Each payment is linked to the sale, so every member can see what they were paid for.

FAQ

Common questions

Which currencies can I hold and send?

Any currency your trade needs, through T57's banking and payment partners.

How long do transfers take?

Payments between T57 members settle in near real time. External transfers depend on the destination and partner network, and the expected time is shown before you confirm.

Can I send personal remittances?

Where partners offer it, verified business members can send remittances for staff. T57 is built for business payments, not personal accounts.
