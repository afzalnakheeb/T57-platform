The problem

AI is only as good as its data

AI is only as good as its data. Most food trade data is incomplete, unverified or locked in private systems, and general-purpose AI models know little about local crops, grades, markets or languages. AI used for decisions about credit, compliance or market access must also be explainable and fair, or it does more harm than good. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

Unreliable data

Unverified listings and scattered records produce unreliable AI.

One-size-fits-all models

Global models miss local conditions, products and languages.

Unaccountable decisions

Black-box AI cannot be trusted with decisions that affect livelihoods.

How the intelligence layer works

Verified data in → Small, local models → Services out → Governance throughout

  1. 01

    Verified data in

  2. 02

  3. 03

    Services out

    Models power matching, pricing, forecasting, risk, compliance, negotiation support, ordering and documents across the platform.

  4. 04

    Governance throughout

Use case · Illustrative example

A forecasting model learns a new crop in Malaysia

T57 adds durian to its Malaysian models as exports grow. Verified trades on the platform, public price data and growers' recorded observations about flowering and weather, shared with consent, train a small local forecasting model. It is tested against past seasons before release, each forecast shows its confidence, and growers and buyers use it to plan harvests and shipments.

FAQ

Common questions

Does T57 use my data to train AI?

T57 uses verified trade data in anonymized, aggregated form to improve its models. Your individual data is never shown to other members, and farmer knowledge is used only with consent.

Why not just use a large general AI model?

Large models are useful for language and documents, and T57 uses them where they fit. For crops, markets and local conditions, small models trained on local data are more accurate, cheaper to run and easier to test and explain.

Can I challenge an AI decision?

Yes. Every adverse decision comes with a reason and a route to contest it, and a person reviews it.
