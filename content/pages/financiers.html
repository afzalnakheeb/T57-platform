Use case · Illustrative example

Escrowed Malaysian rice trade with staged exposure

The opportunity

A regional investment platform wants short-duration exposure to essential food imports in Southeast Asia. Through T57 it reviews a rice supply opportunity linked to a government-supported procurement program in Malaysia, with demand, counterparties and payment structures already assessed.

The structure

Instead of funding the full value at once, the investor commits a first tranche through escrow. Capital is released in stages against verified milestones: supplier confirmation, shipment progress and delivery documentation.

FAQ

Common questions

How does T57 handle disputes and reduce risks for financiers and investors?

T57 reduces risk through its curated club membership model, which ensures vetted, credible counterparties in a high-trust environment. Its virtual trade desk supports execution, manages disputes, oversees documentation, and ensures consistent transaction follow-through for greater investor confidence.

Why is food and agri finance an important opportunity for investors now?

Global food import demand remains enormous: FAO puts the global food import bill at $2.22 trillion in 2025, which underlines the scale and persistence of trade-linked financing needs in food markets.

Is trade finance generally considered a lower risk than other forms of credit exposure?

Yes. The ICC Trade Register highlights that trade, supply chain, and export finance products continue to exhibit low-risk profiles and resilient default performance across challenging market conditions.

Why do financiers need stronger diligence and monitoring tools in agri markets?

Food and agri transactions often involve fragmented supply chains, cross-border counterparties, and climate or logistics volatility, which makes visibility and verification especially valuable. The International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) notes that small-scale producers remain underserved by finance, highlighting that large financing needs go unmet because risk gaps discourage capital.
