T57's architecture solves the integration problem in food trade, by connecting members, partners and authorities to one trade record through secure, region-aware cloud services and open APIs.

The problem

Many systems, many data laws, no common language

Food trade involves banks, insurers, logistics firms, inspectors, customs authorities and thousands of businesses, each with its own systems and rules, in many countries with their own data laws. Most trade platforms are built as closed marketplaces that cannot connect deeply to these parties, or as single-country systems that cannot cross borders. Neither can carry a trade from end to end. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

Many systems, no common language

Partners and members use different formats for the same data.

Data laws by country

Data must often stay in-country, or be shared only on specific terms.

Scale and reliability

Trade infrastructure must stay available and consistent across time zones and markets.

How the architecture works

Cloud foundation → Core services → Integration layer → Intelligence and ledger

  1. 01

    Cloud foundation

    Cloud-native services on Microsoft Azure across regions, with data stored to meet each market's residency rules.

  2. 02

    Core services

    Identity and verification, trade record, workflow, documents and payments orchestration, each a separate service with its own controls.

  3. 03

    Integration layer

    APIs and connectors for partner banks, insurers, logistics providers, inspectors, customs systems and members' ERPs.

  4. 04

    Intelligence and ledger

    AI services and a blockchain layer that read from and anchor the trade record, under strict access rules.

Architecture components

8 parts, one platform

Region-aware data storage

Data hosted to meet local residency requirements. For regulated members and partners, this supports compliance.

Modular services

Identity, trade, documents, payments orchestration and notifications as separate services. For reliability, this limits the impact of any single failure.

Event-driven trade record

Every action is recorded as an event, which builds a complete history. For audit, this means nothing is lost or overwritten.

Partner connectors

Standard connectors for banks, payment institutions, insurers and logistics systems. For partners, this means faster onboarding.

Member APIs

REST APIs and webhooks for ERP, accounting and procurement integration. For enterprises, this means T57 fits their workflow.

Standards-based data

Trade data mapped to recognized standards such as HS codes, Incoterms and electronic trade document formats. For partners, this means interoperability.

Consent and permissions engine

Controls who sees what on each trade. For members, this keeps commercial data private.

Monitoring and resilience

Continuous monitoring, backups and recovery across regions. For members, this means a platform they can rely on.

Use case · Illustrative example

Malaysian bank connects to T57 for trade finance

A Malaysian bank wants to finance more SME food trades but cannot assess small deals cheaply. It connects to T57 through the partner API. Finance requests arrive as structured, verified deal files that its credit system can read directly. Disbursement and repayment events flow back to the trade record, and escrow repays the bank at settlement. The bank's data stays within its own systems and the residency rules it must follow.

FAQ

Common questions

Which cloud does T57 use?

Microsoft Azure, across regions, designed to meet each market's data-residency requirements.

Does T57 offer APIs?

Yes. Members and partners can integrate through REST APIs and webhooks. Documentation is available to approved members and partners.

Can partners keep their data in their own systems?

Yes. Partners exchange only the data each trade needs, under agreement and with consent, and keep their own systems of record.
