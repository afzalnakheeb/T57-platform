The problem

Food trade still runs on calls, spreadsheets and guesswork

Most food trade still runs on phone calls, spreadsheets and experience. Buyers search for suppliers by word of mouth. Prices are guessed from last month's deals. Documents are typed by hand and checked by eye. Farmers plant without knowing what the market will want. General-purpose AI tools do not help much, because they know little about a basmati grade in Punjab, a date harvest in Al-Ahsa or the documents a Malaysian importer needs. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

Expertise only the largest players can afford

Market analysts, compliance teams and trade specialists are out of reach for most food SMEs.

Generic AI that does not know food

Large global models lack local crop, market and regulatory knowledge, and often lack local languages.

AI no one can check

Black-box decisions about credit, compliance or pricing are hard to trust.

Use case · Illustrative example

Jeddah importer, before and after T57 AI

Before

A Jeddah food importer found suppliers through agents, checked prices by phone and had its staff type every document. A sanctions issue with one supplier was discovered only when the bank stopped the payment.

With T57 AI

On T57, AI suggests verified suppliers, benchmarks each quote against the price index, screens every counterparty before the deal, drafts documents from the contract and flags supply risks weeks ahead. The importer's team spends its time deciding, not searching and typing.

Why T57 AI

We believe that AI in food trade must be useful, local and accountable.

FAQ

Common questions

What does "AI-native" mean at T57?

AI is built into every step of the trade, from matching and pricing to compliance and documents, rather than added as a separate tool.

Does AI make decisions about my business?

AI makes suggestions and flags issues. People make decisions, and every adverse decision is reviewed by a person.

What data does T57's AI use?

Verified trade records, public market, trade and climate data, licensed data from partners, and farmer knowledge recorded with consent. Individual members' data is never shared with other members.

Why small, local models?

Food is global but hyper-local. Small models trained for a region, crop and value chain understand local conditions and languages better, cost less to run and are easier to test and explain.
