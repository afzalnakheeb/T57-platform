The problem

Banking that slows trade down

Migrants sent about $729 billion home to low- and middle-income countries in 2025, and digital transfers cost an average of 4.6% compared with 7.3% for non-digital ones (IFAD, Sending Money Home 2026). Food businesses face the same friction at a larger scale. Opening foreign-currency accounts can take weeks, cross-border payments pass through several banks, and smaller firms in some markets struggle to access dollars at all. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

Hard to bank

SMEs and businesses in emerging markets are often refused or delayed by banks.

Costly transfers

Fees and exchange spreads eat into thin food margins.

Limited currency access

In some corridors, businesses cannot easily obtain the currency they need to pay suppliers.

Use case · Illustrative example

Lahore basmati exporter is paid in dirhams and pays freight in dollars

A basmati exporter in Lahore sells to a verified Sharjah importer and books freight with a Dubai forwarder. Through T57's banking partner it receives the importer's payment in dirhams, pays the forwarder in US dollars and converts the balance to rupees at a rate shown upfront. Every movement is matched to the trade, so its accountant reconciles the shipment in minutes.

FAQ

Common questions

Is T57 a bank?

No. T57 connects verified members to licensed banking and payment partners and links their services to the trade record.

Can I keep my existing bank?

Yes. You can keep using your own bank and connect it to T57, or use T57's partners alongside it.

Are digital-asset services available everywhere?

No. They are offered only where the law allows, through regulated partners.
