T57 solves the standoff between buyers who will not pay first and sellers who will not ship first, by holding payment with a licensed escrow partner until agreed milestones are met.

The problem

Buyer and seller both carry the risk

In food trade, someone always carries the risk. If the buyer pays upfront, it risks fraud, short shipment or poor quality. If the seller ships first, it risks late payment or no payment at all. Letters of credit solve this for some deals, but they can be costly and slow for smaller trades and domestic wholesale. Without a trusted middle ground, many good deals never happen.

How it works

Agree milestones → Fund escrow → Release

  1. 01

    Agree milestones

    Buyer and seller agree when funds are released, for example on passed inspection, shipment or delivery, as part of the contract.

  2. 02

    Fund escrow

    The buyer pays into escrow with a licensed partner. The seller can see that the money is there before it ships.

  3. 03

    Release

    As each milestone is recorded on the trade record, funds are released, in part or in full. If there is a dispute, release pauses until it is resolved.

What makes it different

Use case · Illustrative example

Abu Dhabi hotel group buys produce from a verified UAE farm

An Abu Dhabi hotel group agrees a three-month supply of fresh vegetables with a verified farm in Al Ain. The group pays each month's order into escrow with T57's licensed partner. When deliveries are confirmed against the order, payment is released to the farm. When one delivery arrives short, the group raises it on the trade record, release for that delivery pauses, and the difference is settled before the rest is paid.

FAQ

Common questions

Who holds the money in escrow?

A licensed escrow partner. T57 does not hold client funds.

What happens if there is a dispute?

Release pauses automatically, and the dispute is handled under the contract and the partner's rules. Release resumes once it is resolved.
