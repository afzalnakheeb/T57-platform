T57 solves the shortage of capital for food producers and traders, by turning verified food assets into tokenized, traceable investments that lenders and investors can fund through licensed platforms.

The problem

Valuable assets that cannot raise capital

Food businesses own valuable, real assets: harvests under contract, stock in warehouses, invoices from reliable buyers, cold stores and processing lines. But these assets are hard to use as security. Documents can be copied or pledged twice, investors cannot easily check them, and smaller deals are too costly for banks to assess. Meanwhile, investors who want exposure to real food trade have few safe ways in. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

Illiquid assets

Real food assets cannot easily be used to raise money.

Double pledging and fraud

Paper documents can be used as security with more than one lender.

No route for investors

Institutional and qualified investors lack verified, traceable food assets to fund.

How T57 works for you

Verify the asset → Tokenize → Raise funds → Repay and retire

  1. 01

    Verify the asset

    An invoice, warehouse receipt, contract or piece of equipment is verified against the T57 trade record and, where needed, inspected.

  2. 02

    Tokenize

    The asset is recorded as a unique digital token on a tamper-evident ledger, linked to its owner and the underlying trade.

  3. 03

    Raise funds

    Licensed lenders, or investors through licensed fundraising platforms where the law allows, fund against the token.

  4. 04

    Repay and retire

    When the trade settles or the asset is sold, investors are repaid and the token is retired.

Use case · Illustrative example

Al-Ahsa date farm funds a new cold store

A date farm in Al-Ahsa wants to build a cold store to sell year-round instead of at harvest prices. Its forward contracts with verified buyers in Malaysia and India, and the cold store itself once built, are verified on T57 and recorded as tokens. Qualified investors fund the project through a licensed fundraising platform, and repayments flow from escrow as each contracted shipment settles.

FAQ

Common questions

Is this the same as crypto?

No. A tokenized asset is a digital record of a real asset, such as an invoice or a cold store. It is not a currency.

Who can invest?

Licensed lenders, and investors eligible under the rules of the licensed fundraising platform and their own country.

What happens if the trade fails?

The outcome depends on the structure. Funders may have rights to the underlying asset, insurance or guarantees, which are set out before they commit.
