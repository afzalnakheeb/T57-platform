The problem

Gaps in cross-border settlement

In some food trade corridors, bank transfers are slow, dollar access is restricted, or correspondent banks have withdrawn from smaller businesses. Stablecoins and tokenized gold are increasingly used to settle cross-border trade, and regulators in several of T57's markets have set licensing rules for digital-asset service providers. But most food SMEs have no safe, compliant way to use them, and no link between a digital payment and the trade it settles. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

Restricted access to hard currency

Some importers cannot obtain dollars through their banks in time to pay suppliers.

Unregulated channels

Informal crypto transfers expose businesses to fraud, sanctions and legal risk.

No link to the trade

Digital payments made outside the platform cannot be matched to contracts or used as evidence.

How T57 works for you

Use case · Illustrative example

Istanbul nut trader settles with a gold-backed token

An Istanbul hazelnut trader agrees to sell to a verified buyer in a market where both parties prefer real-asset settlement. They agree in the contract to settle in a token backed by allocated physical gold, through T57's regulated partner. The buyer pays, the partner confirms the transfer, and the trader converts part of it to lira at a rate shown upfront. The payment is recorded against the delivery on the trade record.

Why T57

We believe that innovation in payments should make trade safer, not riskier.

Regulated partners only

T57 does not hold client funds or digital assets. All digital-asset services are provided by licensed partners, only where they are lawful.

Optional, never required

Members choose whether to use digital assets. Bank transfers, escrow and letters of credit remain available on every trade.

FAQ

Common questions

Is digital-asset settlement legal in my country?

T57 shows digital-asset options only where they are permitted for both parties under local rules, through licensed partners.

What about price volatility?

Stablecoins and gold-backed tokens are designed to reduce volatility, and partners can convert to local currency at the point of payment.

Are digital-asset payments Shariah-compliant?

Views differ among scholars. Members who need Shariah-compliant settlement should seek guidance and can choose gold-backed tokens or conventional options instead.
