TodayWith T57

T57's price AI solves the missing prices for most traded food, by estimating current prices from scattered market data and verified trades, and explaining why they move.

The problem without AI

Why this is hard without AI

Global benchmarks track the big picture: the FAO Food Price Index averaged 136.0 points in September 2026, up 5.8% on a year earlier (FAO, October 2026). But most food is sold through private deals, in hundreds of grades and pack sizes, with no public price. Data that does exist is patchy, uses different units and currencies, and arrives late. Without AI, building a reliable price for "medjool dates, large, 5 kg cartons, delivered to Kuala Lumpur" is close to impossible.

How the AI works

Data in, a small local model, a person decides

Model typeStatistical and machine-learning models that clean, normalize and combine price signals, estimate prices where trades are sparse, and detect unusual moves. A language model writes plain-language explanations.
Data inputsPublic market and exchange data. Licensed data partners. Anonymized, aggregated prices from verified T57 trades. Freight and currency rates. News and policy events such as export bans and route closures.
OutputA price and range for each product, grade, origin and destination, a confidence level, recent trends, and a short explanation of significant moves.

FAQ

Common questions

How accurate are the AI estimates?

Each price shows a confidence level. Accuracy is measured by comparing estimates with later verified trades, and results are published by product group.

Can the index be manipulated by fake trades?

Only trades between verified members count, outliers are detected and down-weighted, and no single trade can move the index alone.
