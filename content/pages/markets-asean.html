The challenge

The challenge

Southeast Asia is a major supplier of rice, palm oil, seafood, fruit and halal processed food. ASEAN is also moving toward common digital trade rules: negotiations on the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA) concluded in May 2026, with signing targeted for November 2026. Yet many ASEAN producers still rely on intermediaries to reach distant buyers, and cross-border paperwork remains slow. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

Distance from buyers

Producers rarely deal directly with buyers in the Gulf or South Asia.

Paper-based trade

Documents still move by courier and email.

Finance gaps

Small exporters struggle to fund orders while they wait for payment.

FAQ

Common questions

Which ASEAN countries can use T57?

Malaysia is a founding market. Other ASEAN countries will follow.

Can ASEAN producers sell within ASEAN?

Yes. Domestic wholesale is available in Malaysia, and cross-border trade grows as more ASEAN markets join.

How does T57 relate to DEFA?

T57 is an independent platform. Its digital records and consent-based data sharing are designed to fit regional digital trade rules such as DEFA, once in force.
