The problem

Someone always carries the payment risk

Every food trade comes down to one question: who goes first? Buyers fear paying for goods that never arrive or arrive short. Sellers fear shipping goods that are never paid for. Traditional answers, such as advance payment, open account or paper letters of credit, either put all the risk on one side or are too slow and costly for smaller trades. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

One-sided risk

Advance payment and open account leave one party fully exposed.

Slow, paper-heavy protection

Paper letters of credit take days, and document mismatches delay payment.

Payments cut off from the trade

Money moves in one system and goods in another, so disputes are hard to settle.

Use case · Illustrative example

Istanbul confectionery maker ships to a new buyer in Muscat

An Istanbul confectionery manufacturer wins its first order from a verified Muscat distributor and does not want to ship on open account. They agree on T57 to use escrow, with 30% released after pre-shipment inspection and 70% on delivery. FAQ

Common questions

Which payment option should I choose?

Escrow suits most new relationships and domestic wholesale. eLCs suit larger cross-border trades where a bank guarantee is expected. Wallet payments suit trusted, repeat relationships.

Can I combine payment options?

Yes. For example, a deposit can be paid into escrow and the balance under an LC, all on the same trade record.

Who resolves payment disputes?

Disputes are handled under the contract and the licensed partner's rules. Release pauses automatically until they are resolved.
