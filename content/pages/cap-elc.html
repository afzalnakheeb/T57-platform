The problem

Paper LCs: slow and error-prone

Letters of credit protect both sides of a cross-border trade: the seller is paid if it presents the right documents, and the buyer pays only if those documents are presented. But the process is slow. Applications are filled in by hand, terms are copied from contracts, and documents travel by courier between banks. When the documents do not exactly match the LC, payment is delayed. Laws such as the UNCITRAL Model Law on Electronic Transferable Records and the UK Electronic Trade Documents Act 2023 now recognize electronic trade documents, but most food SMEs still work on paper. The key challenges T57 addresses include:

Slow issuance

Manual applications and bank processes take days.

Discrepancies

Small differences between the LC, the contract and the shipping documents delay payment.

Paper and couriers

Physical documents add cost and time, and can be lost or altered.

Use case · Illustrative example

Guntur chilli exporter ships to Kuala Lumpur under an eLC

A dried chilli exporter in Guntur, Andhra Pradesh, agrees to supply a verified Kuala Lumpur spice blender. The blender applies for an eLC on T57 from the accepted contract, and its partner bank approves it. The LC is issued shortly afterwards and the exporter ships. Before presenting, the AI check flags that the certificate of origin shows a different port of loading from the LC. The exporter corrects it, presents digitally, and is paid without a discrepancy fee.

FAQ

Common questions

What does "about 15 minutes" mean?

It is the target time for issuance after the bank has approved your application. Approval follows the bank's normal credit process.

Can my own bank issue the LC on T57?

If your bank is a T57 partner, yes. If not, T57 can connect you to participating banks.

Are electronic LCs legally valid?

Letters of credit are commonly issued and advised electronically between banks. Electronic presentation of trade documents depends on local law, and T57 supports paper presentation where it is still required.
