T57's matchmaking AI solves the slow, contact-driven search for trading partners, by ranking verified members against each request on product, capacity, corridor, compliance and past performance.

The problem without AI

Why this is hard without AI

Food businesses find partners through agents, trade fairs, old contacts and directories full of unverified listings. A buyer may spend weeks finding a supplier who can deliver the right grade, in the right volume, to the right port, and still not know whether that supplier is the producer or one more intermediary. Sellers miss buyers in markets they have never sold to. Good matches exist, but nobody can see them.

How the AI works

Data in, a small local model, a person decides

Model typeA ranking model that combines product and specification matching (using language embeddings of product descriptions) with learned scores for fit and reliability. Regional models handle local product names and grades.
Data inputsThe RFQ or offer (product, grade, volume, packaging, Incoterms, destination, timing). Member profiles, role labels and verified capacity. Corridor and logistics data. Compliance status. The outcomes of past trades, such as completion, on-time delivery and disputes.
OutputA ranked shortlist of verified counterparties, each with the reasons it was suggested, such as "owner-producer, ships to Jeddah monthly, 98% on-time on T57". The member chooses whom to invite.

Use case · Illustrative example

Selangor snack manufacturer finds a new date paste supplier

Before

A snack manufacturer in Selangor spent six weeks calling agents to find a new date paste supplier after its usual source fell short, and still could not confirm who made the product.

With T57 AI

It posts an RFQ on T57, and matchmaking ranks five verified suppliers in Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Two are labeled owner-producer, with halal certificates and recent shipments to Malaysia. It invites three, receives bids within days and awards the contract to a producer in Qassim.
